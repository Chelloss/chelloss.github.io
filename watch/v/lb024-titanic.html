Titanic Could Float With Four Forward Compartments Flooded. Why Did Six Sink Her?

Titanic was built to stay afloat with up to four forward compartments flooded. The iceberg opened six. Here is the hull in 3D: the compartments, the bulkheads, the doors, and why six was too many.

The story

Four forward compartments flooded? She floats.1,2 Her lower decks: sixteen compartments, split by fifteen watertight bulkheads.2,3 Watertight doors could seal them, some closed remotely from the bridge.1,2

The iceberg buckled her starboard plates. Six compartments opened to the sea.1,2 Designed for any two flooded, and some combinations up to four.2,3 But the bulkheads weren't sealed at the top. As the bow sank, water spilled over each one into the next.1,2,3
