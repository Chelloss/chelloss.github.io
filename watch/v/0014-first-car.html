The First Road Trip in a Car Was Made by a Woman, in 1888

On 5 August 1888, Bertha Benz drove Carl Benz's Patent-Motorwagen about 106 km from Mannheim to Pforzheim with her two sons, buying fuel at a pharmacy and fixing the car on the way.

Watch on YouTube Subscribe

The story

You're Bertha Benz. It's August 5th, 1888.1,2 Your husband Carl built this motor car. But he's a poor salesman.1,2 So without telling him, you take your two teenage sons and drive from Mannheim to Pforzheim.1,3

About 106 kilometers. No one has driven a car that far.1,2 In Wiesloch, you buy fuel, a solvent called ligroin, at the town pharmacy.1,3 A fuel line clogs. You clear it with your hat pin.1,2

The brakes wear out, so a cobbler nails on leather. The first brake linings.1,2 Back home, you tell Carl: add a lower gear for the hills.1,2 That pharmacy is now called the world's first filling station.2,3
