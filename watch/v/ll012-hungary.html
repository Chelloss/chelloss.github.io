Hungary's 1946 Banknote Had 20 Zeros. How Do You Fix Money That Broken?

In 1946 Hungary had the worst hyperinflation ever recorded: prices doubled in under a day and rose more than 200 percent a day at the peak; its last pengo notes were worth 100 quintillion pengo. The fix was a new currency: one forint for 400 octillion pengo, 29 zeros gone at once.

The story

Prices doubled in under a day.1,2 Hungary, 1946, just after the Second World War: the worst hyperinflation ever recorded.1,3 Its last pengo notes, in July 1946, were worth 100 quintillion pengo: a one with 20 zeros.1,3

And it got worse: at the peak, prices rose by more than 200 percent every single day.2,3 So how do you fix money that is this broken? The fix: a new currency. One forint was worth 400 octillion old pengo, wiping out 29 zeros at once.1,3
