The Panama Canal Lifts Ships 26 Metres. Where Is the Pump?

The Panama Canal lifts ships 26 metres, three steps up to Gatun Lake, and there isn't a single pump. Here is a lock taken apart in 3D: the concrete chamber, the gates, the valves, the giant tunnels, and what really moves all that water.

The story

A ship climbs 26 metres. No pumps fill the locks.2,3 Each original lock: a concrete box 320 metres long, gates both ends.2,3 Close the gates behind the ship. Open valves deep below.2,3

Through huge tunnels, about 101,000 cubic metres pour into each chamber.2,3 At Gatun, three steps like this lift it into Gatun Lake.1,2 What pushes the water in? No pump. It runs down from the lake. Gravity lifts the ship.2,3
