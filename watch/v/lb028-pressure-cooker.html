In 1835 Darwin's Potatoes Sat on the Fire All Night. They Never Cooked

In March 1835, high in the Andes, Darwin's potatoes stayed hard after a night of boiling. His companions blamed the new pot; Darwin knew thin air lowers water's boiling point. A pressure cooker does the reverse, pushing it to about 121 C.

The story

All night on the fire. Still not cooked.3,4 Charles Darwin, in the Andes, 1835. The water boiled, yet his potatoes stayed nearly as hard as ever.1,3,4 His two companions blamed the pot. It was new.3,4

Darwin knew the culprit: thin air. Up there, water boils below 100 degrees Celsius.1,3 A pressure cooker does the reverse: trapped steam lifts boiling to about 121 Celsius.1,2
