Guinness Calls This 1839 Photo the First Selfie. Here's How He Took It

In autumn 1839, Robert Cornelius pointed a homemade camera at himself in Philadelphia. The daguerreotype, now at the Library of Congress, is often called the first selfie.

Watch on YouTube Subscribe

The story

You're Robert Cornelius. Philadelphia, 1839.2,5 Photography is brand new. Daguerre announced his method in France only that August.4,5 You work in your family's lamp business, and you're an expert at silver plating.2,3

Your camera? A box, with a lens from an opera glass.3,4 Indoors is too dark, so you set up outside, behind the family store.2,4 Uncover the lens, get in front of it, and hold still.3,5

This is the real one, at the Library of Congress. Hair messy, arms crossed, a little off-centre.1,2,4 On the back you wrote: "The first light picture ever taken. 1839."3,6 Guinness calls it the first selfie.2,4

Months later, you open one of America's first photo studios. Not bad for a lamp maker.2,5
