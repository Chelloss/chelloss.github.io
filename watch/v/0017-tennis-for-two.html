People Lined Up for This Video Game in 1958

On 18 October 1958, visitors at Brookhaven National Laboratory lined up to play Tennis for Two, William Higinbotham's tennis game on a five-inch oscilloscope screen, one of the earliest video games.

The story

October 1958. A science lab's open day, and there's a line.1,2 Physicist William Higinbotham thought science exhibits were dull, so he built a game.1,2 The screen is an oscilloscope, just five inches across.2,3

The ball is a dot of light. Turn the dial to aim, press the button to hit.1,2 It's tennis, seen from the side. It's called Tennis for Two.1,2 Hundreds of visitors line up to play.1,2

The next year, you could even play it with the gravity of the Moon, or Jupiter.2,3 It's one of the earliest video games ever made.1,2
